# Digital Exit Design — Darkland

## Core mechanic

The park becomes a six-sided cube route. Each side is a room with one memory shard and one abstract symbol. The player must collect three shards, inspect the symbols, and enter a six-digit code at the Digital Exit. The code is generated from the order in which the player restores the neutral principles, not from any real religious text.

## Respectful symbolism

The game uses six invented glyph names: **Mercy**, **Inquiry**, **Balance**, **Remembrance**, **Care**, and **Choice**. They are universal narrative themes, not labels for Islam, Christianity, Judaism, Buddhism, Hinduism, or any other living tradition. No sacred quotations, scripture pages, religious figures, ritual objects, or claims about believers appear in the game.

The lock is framed as a fictional archive puzzle: the player is not asked to choose a religion. They are asked to assemble a sequence of human values to open a digital passage.

## Exit rule

The initial route exposes three shards. After collection, the Archive House reveals a six-glyph lock. The player clicks or presses the number keys shown by the glyphs in the discovered order. A correct sequence opens the gate; an incorrect sequence clears the entry buffer and explains the reset without punishment.
